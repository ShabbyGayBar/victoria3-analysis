    Supply chains¶

    How many workers and construction points does a supply chain require? Which production configurations supply its inputs? examples/supply_chain_compare.py answers these questions for every producible good, saving the reports, plots, and diagrams under each configuration and then terminal good.

    The supply-chain guide explains the API and graph model. The data dictionary defines the report columns, units, formulas, and failure behavior.

    View examples/supply_chain_compare.py

    Run the comparison¶

    Generate the parsed and production datasets first, then run:

    uv run python -m examples.supply_chain_compare

    To analyze selected goods and save the results to a temporary artifact root:

    uv run python -m examples.supply_chain_compare \
    --datasets-dir artifacts/datasets \
    --output-dir /tmp/supply-chain-comparison \
    --goods automobiles steel

    Each good has a weekly target base value of £100,000, with target quantity calculated as 100_000 / base_price. This is a minimum net-production target. Surplus and coproducts are possible, so equal target value does not imply equal GDP or make goods interchangeable.

    Configuration Objective Technology and additional restrictions
    era_3_construction Minimum whole-system construction Era 3
    era_5_automation Minimum whole-system employment Era 5
    cangshulun Minimum whole-system employment Era 5, CANGSHULUN_BANNED_PMS, and CANGSHULUN_BUILDING_LIMITS

    All configurations use autarky, the shared DEFAULT_BANNED_BGS exclusions, base prices, and continuous building levels. There are no construction budgets or budget sensitivity experiments. The era 3 and era 5 presets change both the objective and technology availability; their difference cannot be attributed to the objective alone. The cangshulun preset shows how its additional PM and building restrictions change an era 5 minimum-employment chain.

    Era availability does not simulate every in-game law, qualification, or state-specific PM restriction. These are model requirements, with no wage costs or profession availability constraints.

    A failed solve produces a configuration-summary row with its requested target, status, and error text, and the script continues to the next configuration and good. Failed configurations have no detailed reports or plots. Unknown goods, duplicate good keys, and invalid base prices raise before any output is written.

    Read one good's results¶

    Filter artifacts/analyses/supply_chains/comparison/summary.csv by good == "automobiles" to compare the three presets for automobiles. Each preset's detailed results live under its own configuration directory.

    The regenerated CSV snapshot gives the following automobile requirements. The input CSVs do not identify a game version, so these numbers describe this snapshot and the assumptions above.

    Configuration Status Employment (workers) Construction (points)
    Era 3 construction Infeasible Unavailable Unavailable
    Era 5 automation Success 125,161 25,217.7
    Cangshulun Success 138,218 27,713.7

    The £100,000 target corresponds to 1,000 automobiles per week. Both successful cases produce £100,000 of weekly value added, or £5.2 million annually, while using different amounts of labor and construction. In this example, whole-system and terminal-chain totals agree; that equality is not a general guarantee of the API.

    Within analyses/supply_chains/era_5_automation/automobiles/, the reports explain where those requirements come from:

    • goods.csv and goods_balance.svg compare supply, consumption, imports, and net production. The terminal production floor is a separate constraint, rather than a population or export buy order.
    • processes.csv identifies the selected building/PM configurations and their levels. flows.csv records process inputs and outputs.
    • workforce.csv and workforce.svg describe employment by profession. Minimizing total employment does not necessarily minimize skilled labor.
    • constraints.csv, bottlenecks.csv, and constraints.svg expose LP diagnostics and useful binding inequalities. Marginals are local and objective-specific, and constraints can have different units.
    • alternatives.csv lists all allowed producer configurations, including inactive ones. The diagrams can prune alternatives for readability.

    Cycles represent real feedback. For example, oil extraction can consume engines while motor industries consume oil. A traversal cutoff does not turn either good into a free resource. Check the quantitative flows rather than inferring requirements from graph depth.

    The regenerated era 5 automobile network contains 16 processes, 16 goods, and 77 edges, with one cyclic component. The allowed graph includes many more alternatives, so a pruned diagram cannot establish their total count.

    Compare contributions¶

    contributions_gdp.svg, contributions_employment.svg, and contributions_construction_cost.svg show leading whole-process contributions to the terminal chain. The same process can matter very differently for labor, construction, and value added.

    For the era 5 automobile chain, automotive assembly employs 70,000 workers and requires 16,000 construction points. The upstream chain therefore accounts for another 55,161 workers. Rubber plantations employ about 16,392 workers but require only 655.7 construction points; oil rigs employ 15,590 workers and require 2,078.7 points. Their relative importance changes between the employment and construction charts. Machinists and engineers together account for 73.9% of chain employment, which illustrates why a small total workforce can still depend heavily on skilled professions.

    Chain totals include whole selected processes, including their coproducts; they do not allocate a factory's workers or construction uniquely to one good. Whole-system totals can also include activity outside the graph-defined chain. Weekly value added is output base value minus intermediate input base cost; annual GDP is 52 times weekly GDP.

It contains one row per good/configuration pair selected in the current run, in requested good order and then configuration order. It retains failures.     The comparison directory contains this summary and three grouped bar charts:

    • pop.svg plots system_employment, the whole-system employed population in workers. It excludes dependents and unemployed people.
    • construction_points.svg plots system_construction_cost in construction points.
    • gdp_per_capita.svg plots system_gdp_per_capita_annual: annual system GDP divided by system employment, in £ per worker per year. Zero employment gives zero, matching the economy model's GDP-per-capita convention.

    All charts use goods as the main horizontal-axis ticks, with configuration ticks beneath the individual bars. Colors identify configurations consistently between charts. A failed solve keeps its tick and shows an × instead of a bar; it is not treated as a zero requirement. SVG width grows with the number of goods so the two tick levels remain readable when zoomed.

    Output inventory¶

    Paths are relative to --output-dir. All text outputs use UTF-8 without a BOM. The comparison writes one detailed result at a time rather than retaining all goods' graphs and snapshots in memory.

    Directory Files
    analyses/supply_chains/era_3_construction/<good>/ Detailed files below for successful solves
    analyses/supply_chains/era_5_automation/<good>/ Same reports, plots, and diagrams
    analyses/supply_chains/cangshulun/<good>/ Same reports, plots, and diagrams
    analyses/supply_chains/<configuration>/ summary.csv, combining that configuration's selected goods
    analyses/supply_chains/comparison/ summary.csv, pop.svg, construction_points.svg, gdp_per_capita.svg, covering all selected goods and presets

    Each successful comparison configuration contains goods.csv, processes.csv, flows.csv, workforce.csv, constraints.csv, bottlenecks.csv, alternatives.csv, supply_chain_recipe.mmd, supply_chain_realised.mmd, network.svg, goods_balance.svg, workforce.svg, constraints.svg, contributions_gdp.svg, contributions_employment.svg, and contributions_construction_cost.svg. Headline metrics and failure rows are stored only in the configuration-level summary.csv and comparison CSVs.

    The configuration summary and comparison CSVs use the public sweep schema, plus configuration, ban_config, and system_gdp_per_capita_annual. The other detailed CSVs retain their public report schemas. Cross-good summaries sit at configuration level. A selected-good run replaces the configuration summaries and combined comparison with that run's selection; other goods' directories remain.
